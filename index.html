Department of Mechanical Engineering · University of Idaho

Computational mechanics for materials and systems under extreme conditions

We build intelligent computational frameworks to predict and engineer how materials, structures and systems perform when pushed to their limits: under impact, at high strain rates, during manufacturing and in reactive environments.

Research

Tougher, smarter and more sustainable performance

Our research program combines particle and mesh-based numerical methods, multiscale modeling and physics-informed AI, run at scale on high-performance computers. Each model we build is meant to answer an engineering question, and we validate against experiments with our national laboratory collaborators.

TougherResisting failure

Impact- and blast-resistant structures, protective materials and fracture across scales.

SmarterPredicting in real time

Digital twins, surrogate models and closed-loop optimization of manufacturing processes.

More sustainableUsing less, wasting less

Energy storage, thermal management and reactive processing for polymer recycling.

Research thrusts · where the lab is headed

THRUST 01

Advanced manufacturing simulation

We will develop GPU-accelerated models of powder-scale mechanics, melt pool dynamics, and sintering and densification, and pair them with physics-informed neural networks to optimize manufacturing processes in a closed loop.

Target applications: additive manufacturing, powder metallurgy, laser processing.

DEMMPMMelt poolSintering
THRUST 02

Material failure and fracture across scales

We aim to connect fracture from atomic bonds through grain-level microstructure to structural response. We will build on molecular dynamics and particle methods and extend them with crystal plasticity and peridynamics.

Target applications: impact- and blast-resistant structures, protection systems, aerospace materials.

Molecular dynamicsPeridynamicsCrystal plasticity
THRUST 03

Thermo-reactive materials and energy systems

We will couple thermal, mechanical and chemical physics to model mechanochemical reactors, phase-change composites for thermal storage, and electrothermal processing for recycling and carbon capture.

Target applications: battery thermal management, solar thermal systems, sustainable manufacturing.

MechanochemistryMultiphysicsPhase change
THRUST 04

Physics-informed AI and data-driven mechanics

We will develop neural operators, physics-informed neural networks, Bayesian uncertainty quantification and data-driven constitutive models that make high-fidelity simulation fast enough for real-time decisions.

Target applications: digital twins, structural health monitoring, inverse design, manufacturing surrogates.

PINNsDeepONetBayesian UQ

Mohammed H. Saffarini, PhD

Assistant Professor of Mechanical Engineering · Principal Investigator

Dr. Saffarini joined the University of Idaho from Oak Ridge National Laboratory, where he was a Postdoctoral Research Associate developing GPU-accelerated computational mechanics for the Department of Energy, including work on mechanochemical polymer recycling. Before that, as a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Missouri, he developed material point method and hybrid FEM–MPM models for an Army research program on explosion-resistant design.

He earned his PhD in Engineering Mechanics at Louisiana State University with a dissertation on multiscale modeling of nano-cellular metals using molecular dynamics. Before his doctorate he spent five years as an offshore methods engineer at SAIPEM, which still shapes how he chooses research problems.

Mahmudul Hasan

PhD Student, Mechanical Engineering

Mahmudul Hasan is a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Idaho, conducting research in the IMPACT Lab. His research centers on computational materials science, mechanics of materials, nanoscale thermal transport and energy materials, with an emphasis on multiscale modeling and simulation. He studies the mechanical behavior and thermal transport properties of advanced materials, particularly two-dimensional MXenes, porous structures and high-entropy alloys. A key part of his work uses molecular dynamics simulations to examine how strain rate, temperature, porosity and defect density govern material performance.

Fully funded PhD positions

We are looking for students who enjoy turning physics into code and code into engineering decisions. Our applications span defense, manufacturing and energy, so there is room for different interests.

What you'll do
